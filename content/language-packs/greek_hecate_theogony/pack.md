## Dialect / variant notes
- **Dialect or register:** Hesiodic epic Ionic, with Aeolic and some
  Boeotian features (Hesiod of Ascra). Typical epic forms in this
  passage include μουνογενής (for μονογενής), κικλῄσκει, ῥηιδίως,
  and uncontracted or traditionally epic morphology. This is not later
  Koine, and it is not the language of the magical papyri.
- **Manuscript variants:** This pack follows the 1914 Loeb/Perseus
  printed text, not a new collation. Two display-order issues in that
  digital text are flagged ⚠️ UNCERTAIN below (425–428 and 429–434).
  At 411 Perseus prints ἢ; many modern texts print ἣ. At 421 the 1914
  English has “Ocean” where this same edition’s Greek has Οὐρανοῦ
  (“Heaven”). A full apparatus (for example in a modern critical
  edition) was not used, because those editions are in copyright;
  West 1966 is cited only, REFERENCE-ONLY.
- **Script or orthography notes:** Standard literary polytonic Greek
  as encoded in the Perseus TEI. Transliteration follows SBL academic
  style (`languages.md`): ē/ō for eta/omega, h for rough breathing, y
  for upsilon, ph/th/ch, iota subscript as āi/ēi/ōi. Accents are not
  marked in the transliteration.
- **Witness date / distance from original:** The poem is traditionally
  dated around 700 BCE (the exact date is debated). The text used here
  is a 1914 printed edition of the medieval Theogony manuscript
  tradition, not a photograph of an archaic or classical witness.

## Uncertainties
> ⚠️ **UNCERTAIN** — authenticity of 411–452 (“Hymn to Hecate”). Why:
> some scholars have treated the passage as a later interpolation
> because of Hecate’s unusual prominence in the Titan genealogy.
> Others, including West 1966, treat it as Hesiodic. Readings on the
> table: authentic Hesiod [^9]; later insertion (a live minority or
> older view). What would settle it: not a single find; the debate is
> literary and textual. Do not state either side as fact. Consulted,
> not quoted: West 1966 [^9]; Clay [^10]. REFERENCE-ONLY.

> ⚠️ **UNCERTAIN** — line 411 genealogy and the form ἢ / ἣ. Why: the
> sentence continues from 409–410, where Asteria is given to Perses;
> “she conceived and bore Hecate” is almost certainly Asteria, daughter
> of Phoibe and Koios, but the transition is part of the interpolation
> debate. Perseus/Evelyn-White prints ἢ at 411; many modern texts print
> ἣ (“and she”). Sense is not seriously in doubt if 409–410 are read as
> context, but the digital breathing and the tightness of the join are
> flagged.

> ⚠️ **UNCERTAIN** — printed line order at 425–428 and 429–434 in this
> edition. Why: the Perseus TEI presents the lines as 425, 427, 426,
> 428 and 429, 434, 430–433. This pack quotes that printed order and
> does not silently rearrange it. Other editions may number or sequence
> the lines differently. What would settle a preferred order: a critical
> apparatus (West 1966 and later, REFERENCE-ONLY).

> ⚠️ **UNCERTAIN** — Evelyn-White’s “Earth and Ocean” at 421. Why: the
> Greek of this same 1914 text is Γαίης τε καὶ Οὐρανοῦ (“Earth and
> Heaven/Sky”), not Ὠκεανοῦ. This pack’s literal and readable layers
> follow the Greek. The publishedTranslation quotes the 1914 English
> as printed. It is unclear whether Evelyn-White followed another
> reading or mistranslated; do not silently “correct” the PD English,
> and do not present “Ocean” as what this Greek says.

> ⚠️ **UNCERTAIN** — later “triple Hecate.” Why: earth, sea, and heaven
> as shares of honor are in this passage; the later artistic and magical
> triple-bodied Hecate is not. Do not read later Hecate-Selene-Artemis
> identifications, or PGM practice, back into Hesiod.

## Ready-to-use summary (Archivist / content bots)
- **One-line takeaway:** In Hesiod’s *Theogony* 411–452, Asteria bears Hecate, and Zeus honors her with a share of earth, sea, and heaven, and with the role of nurse of the young (*kourotrophos*).
- **Safe to quote:** Evelyn-White 1914 (PD), e.g. “And she conceived and bore Hecate whom Zeus the son of Cronos honored above all.” Citation: Evelyn-White 1914, *Theogony* 411–412, via Perseus. Lexicon’s own readable English may also be quoted once Jacqueline accepts this draft; it is not a 1914 text.
- **Pronunciation guide:** Hecate, Greek Ἑκάτη, *Hekatē*, approximately “HEH-kah-tay” in a reconstructed classical pronunciation (labelled as a reconstruction). *Kourotrophos*: “koo-ro-TRO-fos” (nurse/rearer of the young).
- **Do not say as fact:** that this passage is, or is not, a later interpolated “Hymn to Hecate”; that Hesiod already describes the later triple-bodied or magical Hecate; that the 1914 English “Ocean” at 421 is what this Greek text says (the Greek is “Heaven”).
- **Hand-off notes:** Draft for Jacqueline’s review; no READY file; no ritual or spell-work instruction. Homeric Hymn to Demeter and Orphic Hymn 1 remain planned under `greek_hecate_hymns`, not in this pack. West 1966 and Clay are citation-only.

### layer: transliteration
ē d' hypokysamenē Hekatēn teke, tēn peri pantōn

### layer: morphologyNotes
- ἢ / ἣ (411): ⚠️ UNCERTAIN form in this digital text (smooth ἢ vs. expected demonstrative ἣ “and she”). Antecedent: Asteria (409–410), daughter of Phoibe and Koios, wife of Perses.
- ὑποκυσαμένη: aor. mid. part. fem. nom. sg. of ὑποκύομαι, “having conceived.”
- Ἑκάτην: acc. sg., the goddess’s name (LSJ s.v. Ἑκάτη).
- τίμησε: aor. of τιμάω, “honored.” Related noun τιμή (LSJ s.v. τιμή): honor, esteem, office, prerogative — a key word of this whole passage.
- μοῖραν: acc. sg. μοῖρα, “portion, share, allotted part” (LSJ s.v. μοῖρα).
- ἀτρυγέτοιο: gen. sg. ἀτρύγετος, “unharvested, barren,” a traditional epithet of the sea (LSJ s.v. ἀτρύγετος).
- ἔμμορε: 3 sg. perfect of μείρομαι, “has received as one’s portion” (LSJ s.v. μείρομαι).
- τετιμένη: pf. mid./pass. part. of τιμάω, “honored.”

### layer: literalTranslation
And she, having conceived, bore Hecate, whom beyond all
Zeus son of Kronos honored; and he gave to her splendid gifts,
to have a share of earth and of the unharvested sea.
And she also from starry heaven has obtained a portion of honor,
and among the deathless gods she is honored most of all.


### layer: readableTranslation
And she [Asteria] conceived and bore Hecate, whom Zeus, son of Kronos, honored above all others. He gave her splendid gifts: a share of the earth and of the barren sea. She received a portion of honor from starry heaven as well, and among the deathless gods she is honored most highly.

This is Lexicon’s rendering, not Evelyn-White.

### layer: publishedTranslation
And she conceived and bore Hecate whom Zeus the son of Cronos honored above all. He gave her splendid gifts, to have a share of the earth and the unfruitful sea. She received honor also in starry heaven, and is honored exceedingly by the deathless gods.

### layer: publishedTranslationAttribution
Hugh G. Evelyn-White, trans., Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns and Homerica, Loeb Classical Library (London: William Heinemann; New York: Macmillan, 1914), Theogony 411–415. Public domain. Digital source: Perseus Digital Library, https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0130:card=404

### layer: commentary
Immediate context (404–410, not packed as a passage) is Phoibe and Koios as parents of Leto and of Asteria, and Perses taking Asteria as wife. Line 411 continues that genealogy: ⚠️ UNCERTAIN in form (ἢ / ἣ) but not, on the usual reading, in the identity of the mother. The three domains — earth, sea, and starry heaven — are shares of τιμή, not yet the later “triple Hecate” of art and magic (⚠️ UNCERTAIN if anyone equates the two). The unusual fullness of this honor in Hesiod is a standard scholarly observation; authenticity of the whole block 411–452 is debated (⚠️ UNCERTAIN; West 1966 and Clay, REFERENCE-ONLY, consulted not quoted [^9] [^10]).

### layer: morphologyNotes
- ἱλάσκηται: pres. subj. mid. of ἱλάσκομαι, “appease, win favor, propitiate” (LSJ s.v. ἱλάσκομαι).
- κικλῄσκει: epic form of κικλήσκω / καλέω, “calls upon, invokes” (LSJ s.v. κικλήσκω).
- τιμή: again “honor,” here the honor that comes to the mortal worshipper (LSJ s.v. τιμή).
- πρόφρων: “kindly, of good will, well disposed” (LSJ s.v. πρόφρων); γε limits: *if* she is well disposed.
- ὄλβον: ὄλβος, “wealth, prosperity, blessedness” (LSJ s.v. ὄλβος).
- δύναμις: “power, ability” (LSJ s.v. δύναμις).

### layer: literalTranslation
For even now, whenever someone of earth-dwelling humans,
performing fine sacrifices according to custom, seeks to win favor,
he calls upon Hecate. And much honor follows for him
very easily, for whom the goddess, well-disposed at least, will receive the prayers,
and she grants him wealth, since power at any rate is present (with her).


### layer: readableTranslation
For even now, whenever anyone among people on the earth offers fine sacrifices and seeks favor according to custom, he calls on Hecate. Honor comes very readily to the one whose prayers the goddess, if she is well disposed, accepts; and she grants him prosperity, because the power is hers.

This is Lexicon’s rendering, not Evelyn-White.

### layer: publishedTranslation
For to this day, whenever any one of men on earth offers rich sacrifices and prays for favor according to custom, he calls upon Hecate. Great honor comes full easily to him whose prayers the goddess receives favorably, and she bestows wealth upon him; for the power surely is with her.

### layer: publishedTranslationAttribution

### layer: commentary
The passage ties Hecate’s τιμή to ordinary, customary sacrifice, not to later magical instruction. πρόφρων γε is important: the honor is not automatic. No ritual how-to is offered or implied here.

### layer: morphologyNotes

- ἐβιήσατο: aor. mid. of βιάω / βιάζω, “used force on, overpowered.”
- ἀπηύρα: epic aor. “took away.”
- Τιτῆσι: dat. pl. “among the Titans.”
- δασμός: “division, distribution” (LSJ s.v. δασμός).
- γέρας: “gift of honor, privilege, prerogative” (LSJ s.v. γέρας).
- μουνογενής: epic for μονογενής, “only-born, only child” (LSJ s.v. μονογενής).
- τίεται: mid. of τίω, “honors.”

### layer: literalTranslation
For as many as were born of Earth and of Heaven
and obtained an honor, of all these she has a share.
Nor did the son of Kronos at all force her, nor did he take away
whatever she obtained among the Titans, among the earlier gods,
but she holds, as the division first came to be from the beginning,
and a privilege in earth and in heaven and in sea;
nor, because she is only-born, has the goddess obtained less honor,
but still even much more, since Zeus honors her.


### layer: readableTranslation
For of all those born of Earth and Heaven who received an honor, she has a share. The son of Kronos did not force her or take away what she had been allotted among the Titans, the earlier gods. She still holds what was divided from the beginning — a privilege in earth, in heaven, and in the sea. And because she is an only child, the goddess has not received less honor, but even much more, because Zeus honors her.

This is Lexicon’s rendering, not Evelyn-White.

### layer: publishedTranslation
For as many as were born of Earth and Ocean amongst all these she has her due portion. The son of Cronos did her no wrong nor took anything away of all that was her portion among the former Titan gods: but she holds, as the division was at the first from the beginning, privilege both in earth, and in heaven, and in sea. Also, because she is an only child, the goddess receives not less honor, but much more still, for Zeus honors her.

To whom she wishes, she is present in a great way and also benefits;

### layer: originalText

kai tois, hoi glaukēn dyspemphalon ergazontai,

And she is good in the folds, with Hermes, to increase the stock;
and herds of cattle, and wide herds of goats,

from few she makes strong, and from many she has made them less.

Cattle, broad herds of goats, and flocks of woolly sheep she makes strong from a few, or fewer from many, if she is willing in her heart.

## passage: 7

source_ref: 7
passage_id: theogony_448-452
label: Hesiod, Theogony 448–452

### layer: originalText
οὕτω τοι καὶ μουνογενὴς ἐκ μητρὸς ἐοῦσα
πᾶσι μετ᾽ ἀθανάτοισι τετίμηται γεράεσσιν.
θῆκε δέ μιν Κρονίδης κουροτρόφον, οἳ μετ᾽ ἐκείνην
ὀφθαλμοῖσιν ἴδοντο φάος πολυδερκέος Ἠοῦς.
οὕτως ἐξ ἀρχῆς κουροτρόφος, αἳ δέ τε τιμαί.

### layer: transliteration
houtō toi kai mounogenēs ek mētros eousa
pasi met' athanatoisi tetimētai geraessin.
thēke de min Kronidēs kourotrophon, hoi met' ekeinēn
ophthalmoisin idonto phaos polyderkeos Ēous.
houtōs ex archēs kourotrophos, hai de te timai.

### layer: morphologyNotes
- μουνογενὴς ἐκ μητρὸς: “only-born from her mother,” picking up 426.
- τετίμηται γεράεσσιν: pf. mid. “has been honored with privileges” (γέρας, LSJ s.v.).
- κουροτρόφον / κουροτρόφος: “nurse, rearer of the young” (LSJ s.v. κουροτρόφος). The epithet is given by Zeus and said to hold “from the beginning.”
- πολυδερκέος Ἠοῦς: “much-seeing Dawn” (Ἠώς).
- οἳ μετ᾽ ἐκείνην: “who after that (day/time)” — so EW; the demonstrative is slightly compressed. ⚠️ UNCERTAIN in exact reference (that day, or after her), though “from that time on” is the usual sense.
- αἳ δέ τε τιμαί: “and these (are her) honors,” closing the catalogue.

### layer: literalTranslation
So then, even being only-born from her mother,
she has been honored among all the deathless ones with privileges.
And the son of Kronos made her a nurse of the young, who after that (day)
saw with their eyes the light of much-seeing Dawn.
So from the beginning she is a nurse of the young, and these are her honors.


### layer: readableTranslation
So then, even as her mother’s only child, she has been honored with privileges among all the deathless gods. And the son of Kronos made her nurse of the young — of those who, from that day on, saw with their eyes the light of all-seeing Dawn. So from the beginning she is a nurse of the young, and these are her honors.

This is Lexicon’s rendering, not Evelyn-White.

### layer: publishedTranslation
So, then, albeit her mother's only child, she is honored amongst all the deathless gods. And the son of Cronos made her a nurse of the young who after that day saw with their eyes the light of all-seeing Dawn. So from the beginning she is a nurse of the young, and these are her honors.

### layer: publishedTranslationAttribution
Hugh G. Evelyn-White, trans., Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns and Homerica, Loeb Classical Library (London: William Heinemann; New York: Macmillan, 1914), Theogony 448–452. Public domain. Digital source: Perseus Digital Library, https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0130:card=404

### layer: commentary
κουροτρόφος is the closing epithet, not a later magical title. “From the beginning” ties the nursing role to the same archē as the Titanic division of honors. The interpolation debate (⚠️ UNCERTAIN) includes whether this coda is integral. Do not treat κουροτρόφος here as ritual instruction.
