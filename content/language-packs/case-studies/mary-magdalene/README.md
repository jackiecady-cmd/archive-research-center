# Case study: Mary Magdalene

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

Historical and linguistic only. **No ritual instruction, no confessional
apologetic, and no modern bloodline or Grail claim treated as evidence.**
Linked from [`../yeshua/`](../yeshua/), [`../gnostic/`](../gnostic/), the
[`../pantheons/`](../pantheons/) related-links list, and the case-studies
index. Shared packs (`coptic_gospel_mary`, `coptic_gospel_thomas`,
`coptic_pistis_sophia`, and related tractates) are **one top-level folder
each**, listed in more than one `packs.md`. Do not duplicate a pack.

## Scope

- **Canonical Gospels (Greek).** Mark 15–16 (presence at the crucifixion
  and burial; the empty-tomb visit; the longer ending at 16:9–20 is a
  live text-critical problem and must be flagged ⚠️ UNCERTAIN, not
  silently used as Mark). Luke 8:2 (Mary called Magdalene, from whom
  seven demons had gone out) and the related Lukan passion and
  resurrection notices. John 20 (the tomb, “rabbouni,” the first
  appearance in that Gospel). Matthew 27–28 belongs in the same
  canonical picture but is not the first focus of
  `greek_magdalene_canonical`. Recent Bible versions (NIV, NRSV, and
  the like) are REFERENCE-ONLY; prefer SBLGNT (CC BY 4.0) or a named
  public-domain Greek text, with a PD English (for example KJV) only as
  a published-translation layer if one is used.
- **Gospel of Mary.** Berlin Codex 8502 (Papyrus Berolinensis 8502,
  Coptic) and the Greek fragments P.Oxyrhynchus 3525 and P.Rylands 463.
  One shared pack, `coptic_gospel_mary`, also listed under `../gnostic/`.
  Coptic and Greek *wording* is not under copyright; photographs and
  modern editions and English (King, Tuckett, and others) are
  REFERENCE-ONLY until a rights-cleared transcription is named.
- **Gospel of Philip**, Nag Hammadi Codex II,3. The word *koinōnos*
  (“companion,” “partner”; sometimes sensationalized as “wife”) and the
  lacuna at 63:34–36 (often restored as a kiss “on the [mouth?]”) are
  both ⚠️ UNCERTAIN and must not be stated as a marriage claim. Planned
  pack: `coptic_gospel_philip_magdalene`, also listed under `../gnostic/`.
  Modern NHL English is REFERENCE-ONLY.
- **Pistis Sophia** and the **Dialogue of the Savior** (NHC III,5), in
  which Mary is a prominent interlocutor. Cross-list
  `coptic_pistis_sophia` and `coptic_dialogue_savior` with `../gnostic/`.
  Mead 1896 English may be PD with a quality warning; modern Coptic
  editions remain a clearance check.
- **Gospel of Thomas 114** (Peter’s objection; the “make her male”
  saying). Cross-list the existing planned pack `coptic_gospel_thomas`.
  Interpretation is debated and must be flagged, not resolved by
  preference.
- **Gregory the Great, Homily 33** (delivered 21 September 591 in the
  usual dating). The Western conflation of Mary Magdalene with Mary of
  Bethany and with the anonymous anointer / “sinner” of Luke 7. Planned
  pack: `latin_gregory_homily33`. Use a PD Latin text (for example PL)
  and a PD English if one is named. This is reception history, not
  first-century evidence.
- **Liturgical revisions, not language packs.** The 1969 revision of
  the General Roman Calendar distinguished Mary Magdalene in the
  liturgy from the Lukan “sinner” and from Mary of Bethany, undoing
  Gregory’s conflation for Roman liturgical use. In 2016 the Memorial
  was elevated to a Feast (Congregation for Divine Worship decree,
  3 June 2016). Official modern Vatican documents are typically
  copyrighted: cite and link; do not paste them into a PDF; **never
  claim an official Vatican partnership**.
- **Medieval legends.** The *Golden Legend* (*Legenda aurea*) of
  Jacobus de Voragine, from a public-domain Latin or English (Caxton
  or another pre-1929 printing — Ryan 1993 and other modern versions
  are REFERENCE-ONLY), and the Provence traditions (Saintes-Maries-
  de-la-Mer, Sainte-Baume, Vézelay). These are medieval narrative,
  not first-century evidence. A possible later pack is
  `latin_golden_legend_magdalene`.
- **Modern Grail / bloodline claims, as non-evidence.** *Holy Blood,
  Holy Grail* (Baigent, Leigh, and Lincoln, 1982) and *The Da Vinci
  Code* (Brown, 2003) are modern reception (the latter is fiction).
  They are all-rights-reserved, **not evidence** for an ancient
  marriage, child, or bloodline, and they are not to be packed as
  primary sources.
- **The “Gospel of Jesus’ Wife.”** A modern fake, now treated as a
  **forgery** in the scholarly literature (typographical dependence
  on a modern edition of Thomas, and related problems). Record it as
  a flagged forgery, like the *Book of Veles* in the Slavic case
  study. Do not open a primary pack for it.

Out of scope until separately commissioned: systematic theology,
devotional lives of the saint used as if they were first-century
reportage, and modern ritual or channeled Magdalene material. Those
may be logged later as practice or reception context, never as
evidence for what Mark, John, or the Gospel of Mary says.

## Method

- Date the witness, the language, and the edition separately. A
  fourth-century Coptic book is not a first-century report; a 591
  homily is not a Gospel; a 1982 bestseller is not a source for
  antiquity.
- Academic consensus and minority positions are labelled as such.
  Faith claims (resurrection witness, saintly cult, later mystical
  readings) are reported as claims of a source or tradition, not as
  facts established by the pack.
- Tag every source in this folder as one of: **canonical**, **early
  extra-canonical**, **patristic conflation**, **liturgical
  revision**, **medieval legend**, **modern reception**, or
  **forgery**. Do not mix tags silently.
- Copyrighted modern editions and translations are REFERENCE-ONLY
  and will not appear in PDFs. Only PD, CC-BY, or CC-BY-SA wording
  may go into a pack PDF once a pack is READY.
- Do not say as fact: that *koinōnos* in Philip means “wife”; that
  the lacuna at Philip 63:34–36 can be restored with certainty; that
  Gregory’s three-Mary conflation is already in the canonical
  Gospels; that Provence legends preserve first-century travel; that
  *Holy Blood, Holy Grail* or *The Da Vinci Code* is historical
  evidence; or that the “Gospel of Jesus’ Wife” is an ancient text.
