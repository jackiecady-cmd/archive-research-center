# Case study: Mesopotamian pantheon

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

This is the first tradition-level folder under the pantheons index
(`content/language-packs/case-studies/pantheons/README.md`). It is
historical and linguistic. It does not teach ritual practice.

## Scope

A starting dossier on major Sumerian and Akkadian deities and the
compositions already flagged as priority texts, beginning with:

- **Inanna / Ishtar**, including Inanna’s Descent (already proposed as a
  first-wave pack; see the source catalog and earlier priority-text notes)
  and the Akkadian Descent of Ishtar.
- **Enki / Ea**, including the Sumerian Enki and Ninhursag tradition.
- **Enlil**, including Enlil and Ninlil, and the roles of Enki and Enlil
  in the Akkadian Atra-hasis flood narrative.

Later deities and compositions (An / Anu, Ninurta, Ereshkigal, Marduk,
Ashur, *šēdu*, *lilitu*, Pazuzu, Lamashtu, and others) can be added as
packs and linked from here, from `../demonization/`, or both.
Lilith / *lilītu* now has its own case study,
[`../lilith/`](../lilith/), and a first draft pack,
`sumerian_lilith`. Do not
collapse a Sumerian hymn, an Old Babylonian narrative, and a first-
millennium Standard Babylonian recension into one “the myth.”

Primary source routes: **CDLI** (https://cdli.earth/; text generally
reusable with attribution; images often NC / permission), **ORACC**
(CC BY-SA 3.0), and **ETCSL** (Oxford editors’ copyright 1998–2006 —
**REFERENCE-ONLY**, not for PDFs, though it may be used to *evaluate* AI
translations). See `content/language-packs/source-catalog.md`.

## Method

- Prefer a named tablet or composite with a CDLI / ORACC citation over an
  unsourced English retelling.
- ETCSL English and composites stay REFERENCE-ONLY until permission exists.
- Date language and recension (Sumerian, Old Babylonian Akkadian, Standard
  Babylonian, etc.) in every pack.
- Modern devotional Inanna or “dark goddess” readings are modern
  perspectives, not evidence for a Sumerian text.
