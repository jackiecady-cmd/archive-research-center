# Case study: Hindu traditions

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

Part of the pantheons index. Historical and linguistic only. **No ritual
or tantric instruction.** “Hindu” here is a convenient umbrella for
dated Sanskrit (and, later, vernacular) sources: Vedic, epic, purāṇic,
and selected devotional or tantric *texts as texts*. Living practice is
not a pack source unless a community-licensed document is named.

## Scope

- **Rigveda** and other Vedic material, from a named Sanskrit text
  (GRETIL or a PD edition — **licence check**) and a PD English such as
  Griffith where it is used as a historical published layer (quality
  warning).
- **Bhagavad Gītā** (Mahābhārata), same rule: named Sanskrit; 19th-c.
  PD English if used; recent translations REFERENCE-ONLY.
- **Devī Māhātmya** (in the *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*) and other purāṇic
  goddess texts, including Kālī / Durgā passages, for the goddess
  series, from rights-cleared sources only. The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* is
  in scope as context, not as a first-wave pack until a PDF-OK text is
  named.
- **Mahābhārata** and **Rāmāyaṇa**. Kisari Mohan Ganguli’s Mahābhārata
  English is US PD. The BORI critical edition (and other 20th-c.
  critical texts, including the Baroda Rāmāyaṇa) need a **licensing
  check** and are not assumed PDF-OK.
- **Upaniṣads**, with Max Müller’s *Sacred Books of the East* English
  as a PD historical published layer (quality warning).
- Later tantric or āgamic works only as **historical texts**, never as
  how-to, and only when a PDF-OK edition exists.

Source routes already in the catalog: GRETIL (typically “free for
scholarly use,” **not automatically CC** — check), Muktabodha, SARIT
(site was unstable here), Cologne Sanskrit lexica (usually
REFERENCE-ONLY / LINK-ONLY for dumps).

## Method

- Date language and recension (Vedic, Classical Sanskrit, later).
- Modern devotion and Western occult “Hindu” borrowings are modern
  perspectives, not evidence for a Ṛgvedic hymn.
- The Iranian *daeva* / Indic *deva* inversion is noted under
  [`../zoroastrian/`](../zoroastrian/), not used to rewrite these hymns.
