# Case study: Celtic (Irish, Welsh, Gaulish)

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

Part of the pantheons index
(`content/language-packs/case-studies/pantheons/README.md`), and of
the goddess series for the Morrígan. Historical and linguistic only.
**No ritual instruction** and no modern-pagan how-to.

## Scope

- **Irish.** *Lebor Gabála Érenn*; the *Táin* (*Táin Bó Cúailnge*);
  *Cath Maige Tuired* (the Battles of Mag Tuired); the Tuatha Dé Danann;
  the Morrígan in those narratives and in related early Irish poetry.
  Medieval Irish literature is not a Bronze Age “Celtic pantheon”
  handbook; euhemerism, Christian compilation, and later recension must
  be flagged.
- **Welsh.** The Mabinogi / *Mabinogion* and the Book of Taliesin.
  Lady Charlotte Guest’s English (19th c.) is US PD; the Middle Welsh
  must come from a named, rights-cleared edition. Guest is a historical
  translation, not the last word.
- **Gaulish.** The inscriptions, beginning with Larzac and Chamalières,
  and the *Recueil des inscriptions gauloises* (RIG). These are short
  and often difficult; uncertainties stay on the page. Do not read the
  medieval Irish gods back into a Gaulish lead tablet.
- **Classical notices** of druids and Gallic religion (Caesar, *Bellum
  Gallicum*; Pliny, *Naturalis Historia*), kept labelled as Roman
  ethnography, not as native Celtic liturgy.

Source route for much Irish and some Welsh: the CELT corpus at
University College Cork (https://celt.ucc.ie/). **Licence is per text
and must be checked** before any PDF; some files are editorial and may
be NC or rights-reserved.

## Method

- Date the language (Old Irish, Middle Irish, Middle Welsh, Gaulish) and
  the manuscript, not an undated “Celtic myth.”
- The Morrígan, the Tuatha Dé, and Welsh figures attract modern
  devotional readings; those are modern perspectives, not evidence for
  the medieval text.
