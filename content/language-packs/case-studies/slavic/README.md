## Scope

Figures often grouped as a Slavic “pantheon,” kept in their actual
source layers:

- **Perun, Veles (Volos), Mokosh**, and other names from early medieval
  testimony, especially the **Primary Chronicle** (*Povest’ vremennykh
  let*) and the notice of Vladimir’s pantheon (c. 980).
- **Morana / Marzanna**, **Baba Yaga**, and related figures, which are
  largely later folkloric and must not be read back into the Chronicle
  as if they stood in Vladimir’s list.
- **Chernobog** (and **Svantevit** / Sventovit), chiefly from Latin
  ethnography: Helmold, *Chronica Slavorum*; also Saxo Grammaticus on
  Arkona, Thietmar of Merseburg, and earlier notices such as Procopius.
  Chernobog is thinly attested; do not inflate him from modern lists.

Other sources in scope: Novgorod **birch-bark letters** (gramoty.ru and
named editions; licence **check**); Alexander Afanasyev’s folk-tale
collections (**PD**); the **Zbruch idol** as an archaeological object
(interpretation is debated; images follow the museum’s terms, usually
LINK-ONLY).

**Forgery flag (required).** The *Book of Veles* (*Velesova kniga*) is a
**modern forgery**. It is not a medieval Slavic source and must not be
used as one. Neo-pagan systematizations built on it, or on unchecked
god-lists, are modern perspectives (and often unreliable), not evidence.

## Method

- Separate Chronicle testimony, German and Danish Latin ethnography,
  Byzantine notices, birch-bark documents, 19th-c. folklore, and
  archaeological objects.
- A 19th-c. tale about Baba Yaga does not attest a pre-Christian cult
  on its own.
