# Case study: Yeshua

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

## Scope
A non-biased historical and linguistic dossier on the name and the earliest
texts connected with Jesus of Nazareth, including:

- The Hebrew / Aramaic name יֵשׁוּעַ (*Yeshua*) and its relation to
  יְהוֹשֻׁעַ (*Yehoshua*, Joshua), including post-exilic biblical usage
  (Ezra, Nehemiah, Chronicles) and later Jewish and Christian forms.
- The Greek New Testament (Koine Ἰησοῦς, *Iēsous*): morphology, variant
  spellings, and how the name is used in a critical text (SBLGNT) and in
  early witnesses (for example Codex Sinaiticus, once terms for the
  transcription are confirmed).
- The Syriac Peshitta (ܝܫܘܥ, *Ishoʿ* / *Yeshua*) as an early Aramaic
  Christian witness, from a rights-cleared edition.
- Sahidic Coptic witnesses, including New Testament portions and the
  Gospel of Thomas (Nag Hammadi Codex II), with modern English Nag Hammadi
  translations treated as REFERENCE-ONLY. Thomas saying 114 is also
  listed from [`../mary-magdalene/`](../mary-magdalene/); the pack
  `coptic_gospel_thomas` stays a single top-level folder.
- Mary Magdalene in the canonical Gospels and in related early texts
  is indexed under [`../mary-magdalene/`](../mary-magdalene/), not
  duplicated here. The planned pack `greek_magdalene_canonical` (Mk
  15–16, Lk 8:2, Jn 20) may be cross-listed from this folder when
  work starts.
- External Greco-Roman references often cited in historical Jesus research:
  Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.63–64, the *Testimonium Flavianum*, and
  20.200), Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44), Pliny the Younger (*Ep.* 10.96–97).
  Authenticity and later interpolation, especially for the *Testimonium*,
  are a live scholarly debate and will be flagged, not smoothed over.
- Dead Sea Scrolls as **linguistic and historical context** for late Second
  Temple Judaism, not as texts about Jesus. No Scroll is treated as a
  Christian document in this case study.

Out of scope until separately commissioned: systematic theology, confessional
apologetics or polemic, and modern channelled or devotional “Yeshua” material.
Those may be logged later as practice context, never as evidence for what a
first- or second-century text says.

## Method
- Academic consensus and minority scholarly positions are labelled as such.
  Faith claims (messiahship, resurrection, divinity) are reported as claims
  of a source or tradition, not as facts established by the pack.
- Every pack names its edition, licence, and the date of the witness used.
- Copyrighted translations (recent Bible versions, Lamsa, modern Nag Hammadi
  English) are REFERENCE-ONLY and will not appear in PDFs.
