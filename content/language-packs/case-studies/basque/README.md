# Case study: Basque

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack

Part of the pantheons index and, for Mari, the goddess series.
Historical and linguistic only. **No ritual or sorginak how-to.**

## Scope

- Folklore figures, kept in dated collections and not inflated into an
  ancient canon: **Mari**, **Sugaar / Sugoi**, **Basajaun**, **lamiak**,
  and the **akelarre** (the witches’ sabbath of later accusation and
  folklore). These are first of all folkloric and early-modern terms.
- **Aquitanian** inscriptions and onomastics (an ancient language
  closely related to the Basque substrate), as the earliest linguistic
  evidence. These are short and often difficult; uncertainties stay
  flagged. They do not automatically attest later folklore figures.
- **Zugarramurdi** trials of **1609–1610** and the **Logroño auto-da-fé**,
  including the reports of Inquisitor **Alonso de Salazar Frías**, as
  historical records of accusation, procedure, and scepticism, not as a
  recovered pagan liturgy.
- Pierre de Lancre, *Tableau de l’inconstance des mauvais anges et
  démons* (1612, PD; Gallica). Labourd / Basque-related demonological
  report, historical/textual only.
- Bernard Etxepare, *Linguae Vasconum Primitiae* (1545, PD), the first
  printed book in Basque — a language witness, not a mythographic
  handbook.
- José Miguel de Barandiaran (folklore and ethnography): **copyright
  check**. He died in 1991, so his work is generally still in copyright
  in life+70 jurisdictions until the end of 2061; treat as
  REFERENCE-ONLY until a specific pre-1929 or licensed item is named.
- Modern Basque literature and neo-pagan systematizations are modern
  perspectives. Early attestation of a “pantheon” is **thin**; do not
  inflate it.

## Method

- Keep Aquitanian epigraphy, early-modern trial records, 19th–20th-c.
  folklore, and contemporary reconstruction in separate layers.
- Licence and language per source (Basque, Spanish, French, Latin).
- Living-tradition and community sensitivity applies to sacred names
  and to trial-victim history.
