# Case study: Satan and Lucifer

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

This dossier treats **two distinct figures** with distinct roots:

1. the **adversary / accuser** lineage (Hebrew *śāṭān* / *ha-satan*,
   Greek *Satanas* and *diabolos*);
2. the **morning-star** lineage (Hebrew *hēlēl ben-šāḥar*, Greek
   *heōsphoros* / *phōsphoros*, Latin *lucifer*).

A third, separate section documents **conflation**: who later merged the
two, when, and why. Cross-links: [`../demonization/`](../demonization/),
[`../goetia/`](../goetia/), [`../egregores/`](../egregores/) (Enochic
Watchers), and the pantheons index. Persian *Ahriman* / Angra Mainyu
belongs in [`../zoroastrian/`](../zoroastrian/); influence claims are a
flagged scholarly debate, not a settled derivation, and apply only to
the adversary lineage.

Historical and research-only. **No ritual or spell-work instruction.**
Modern movements are recorded as historical subjects, not as endorsements
or exposés.

## Style rule (required)

**Do not use “Satan” and “Lucifer” interchangeably anywhere in this
folder or in packs linked from it.** They are not synonyms in the root
sources. Every source, quotation, heading, and pack must be **tagged**
for which figure it actually refers to:

- **adversary lineage** — *śāṭān* / *ha-satan* / *Satanas* / *diabolos*
  (and closely related figures a source itself names, such as Mastema or
  Belial);
- **morning-star lineage** — *hēlēl* / *heōsphoros* / *phōsphoros* /
  Latin *lucifer* as “morning star” or light-bearer, including Roman
  astronomical *Lucifer* and Lucifer of Cagliari;
- **conflation** — a dated author or tradition that *identifies* the
  morning-star figure with the adversary.

If a later writer treats them as one being, say so as **that writer’s
conflation**, not as the meaning of Job, Isaiah, or 2 Peter. Folk
etymologies and unattested name-equations are marked **⚠️ UNCERTAIN**
or rejected when they have no linguistic basis. Do not write “Satan
(Lucifer)” or “Lucifer, i.e. Satan” as if the equation were original.

## Scope

### A. Adversary lineage (*śāṭān* / *ha-satan* / *Satanas*)

- Hebrew הַשָּׂטָן (*ha-satan*, “the accuser / adversary”) in Job 1–2 and
  Zechariah 3; the article matters. Numbers 22:22, where *śāṭān* is the
  angel of the LORD standing as an adversary, is in scope and must not
  be silently omitted.
- 1 Chronicles 21:1 (a *śāṭān* incites David’s census; whether this is
  a title or a proper name is debated) vs. 2 Samuel 24:1 (the LORD’s
  anger): a source-critical and theological problem, not a problem to
  be harmonized away.
- LXX διάβολος (*diabolos*) as a rendering of this adversary vocabulary;
  Aramaic *sāṭānā* / *satana*; Greek Σατανᾶς (*Satanas*) in the New
  Testament.
- Related Second Temple figures, kept distinct unless a source itself
  identifies them: Mastema in *Jubilees*; Belial in the Qumran War
  Scroll (and related texts); Samael in later Jewish tradition; Enochic
  Watchers (see `../egregores/`). None of these is *hēlēl* or Latin
  *lucifer* unless a cited text makes that identification.
- Proposed **Persian / Ahriman** influence on later adversary
  traditions: a scholarly debate, to be linked to a future zoroastrian
  dossier, not treated as a settled derivation of *ha-satan*.

### B. Morning-star lineage (*hēlēl* / *lucifer*)

- Isaiah 14:12, הֵילֵל בֶּן־שָׁחַר (*hēlēl ben-šāḥar*). The passage is
  a taunt of a fallen tyrant (the king of Babylon in context). It does
  not use *śāṭān*.
- LXX ἑωσφόρος (*heōsphoros*); Vulgate *lucifer* (in function:
  “morning star,” light-bearer). These are translations of the
  morning-star image, not of *ha-satan*.
- Ugaritic Šaḥar and related Athtar / morning-star parallels: possible
  background, **not** a simple identity. Flag over-confident equations
  ⚠️ UNCERTAIN where they outrun the evidence.
- Roman *Lucifer* as the morning star (the planet Venus), in Latin poetry
  and astronomy, independent of Isaiah and independent of the adversary.
- 2 Peter 1:19: Greek φωσφόρος (*phōsphoros*); the Latin tradition can
  call **Christ** *lucifer* (morning star). This verse is in the
  morning-star lineage. It does not name *Satanas*.
- Lucifer of Cagliari (4th-c. bishop): a historical person named with
  the morning-star word, not a demonological title and not the
  adversary.

### C. Conflation — who merged them, when, and why

A separate historical problem: how the morning-star taunt of Isaiah 14
was applied to the adversary. Document each witness as **conflation**,
with date and motive (typology, fall-of-the-proud, preaching, poetry),
not as if the merge were already present in the Hebrew.

In scope, as a dated chain (editions and exact passages to be fixed when
packs open):

- **Origen** — interpretive application of the Isaiah morning-star fall
  to a rebellious spiritual being.
- **Tertullian** — related early Latin reception.
- **Jerome’s Vulgate** — the Latin word *lucifer* at Isaiah 14:12 made
  the morning-star verse easy to cite in Latin; Jerome’s own
  commentaries and later use of that wording are part of the conflation
  history. The vocabulary of the verse itself remains morning-star
  language (see section B) and is also used of Christ in 2 Peter 1:19.
- **Augustine** — further Latin theological identification.
- **Gregory the Great** — including the *Moralia in Iob* and the
  medieval afterlife of that reading.
- **Medieval reception** more broadly (commentaries, drama, preaching).
- **Milton**, *Paradise Lost* (1667/1674, **PD**) — a major English
  literary working-out of the already conflated figure; tag the poem as
  **conflation**, not as evidence for Job or for Isaiah 14.
- Romantic writers (Blake, Shelley, Byron) and later literary
  “Satanism”: usually the adversary under the name Satan, sometimes
  using Lucifer as well; tag by the name each text actually uses, and
  note conflation where an author treats them as one.

### Modern movements (historical subjects only; tag the name they use)

- Movements and works that name **Satan**: the Church of Satan (Anton
  LaVey, 1966 — publications **in copyright, REFERENCE-ONLY**); The
  Satanic Temple; Carducci, *Inno a Satana* (PD).
- Movements and works that name **Lucifer**: Luciferianism; Leland,
  *Aradia* (1899, PD), in which Lucifer is a light-bearer figure in a
  late-19th-c. construction, not *ha-satan*.

These do not interpret Job or Isaiah for the philological packs.

## Method

- Do not read a conflating Father, Milton, or LaVey back into Job or
  Isaiah.
- Academic disagreement (Chronicles vs. Samuel; Ahriman influence;
  Ugaritic parallels; how early the conflation begins) is flagged, not
  resolved by preference.
- Every pack names its edition, licence, the date of the witness, and
  the figure-tag (adversary / morning-star / conflation).
- Copyrighted modern editions and all Church of Satan / Temple
  publications are REFERENCE-ONLY and will not appear in PDFs.
