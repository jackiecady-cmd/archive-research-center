# Case study: Alpine folklore, inscriptions, and witch-trial texts

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

Linked from the pantheons index as a regional dossier (Alpine beings,
Raetic epigraphy, early-modern trials, and later folklore collections),
not as a single ancient “Alpine pantheon.” Historical and textual only.
**No ritual or spell-work instruction**, and no modern Krampus- or
witchcraft-practice how-to.

## Scope

- **Perchta**, **Krampus**, and the **Wild Hunt** as folklore and
  historical-attestation problems: dated texts and collections first,
  modern festival culture labelled as modern.
- **Raetic inscriptions** of the Alps. Raetic is not Ladin; the two must
  not be collapsed. Decipherment is limited; uncertainties stay flagged.
- **Valais (Wallis) witch trials of 1428**, beginning with Hans Fründ’s
  report. The requested slug is `latin_frund_valais_1428`; the report is
  generally discussed as an Early New High German witness. Confirm the
  language of the named edition when the pack opens, and retitle the slug
  if needed.
- *Malleus Maleficarum* (late 15th c.), as a historical treatise on
  witchcraft prosecution, **PD** in the Latin text. Later English
  (including Montague Summers, 1928) needs a printing-level PD check
  (1928 US works may now be PD; confirm). This pack is not a manual.
- Jacob Grimm, *Deutsche Mythologie* (PD), as 19th-c. scholarship and
  a source-mine for Perchta, the Hunt, and related figures — useful, and
  often outdated; treat as Grimm, not as medieval evidence.
- **Ladin / Dolomite legends**, including Karl Felix Wolff. **Copyright
  check:** Wolff died in 1966, so his work is generally still in
  copyright in life+70 jurisdictions until the end of 2036; some early
  printings may be US PD if pre-1929. Until cleared, **REFERENCE-ONLY**.

## Method

- Keep inscription, trial record, demonological treatise, Romantic
  folklore, and living festival practice in separate layers.
- Early-modern witch-trial texts are evidence of accusation and belief,
  not of a recovered pagan cult, unless a specific claim is argued and
  sourced.
