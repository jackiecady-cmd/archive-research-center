# Case study: Demonization

Umbrella index only. Packs will live at `/workspace/language-packs/<slug>/`
and are listed in `packs.md`. Status of those packs: **planned**. No pack
folders have been created yet. No translations have been started.

A broader **pantheons** index (Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Canaanite/Ugaritic,
Greek, Roman, Norse, Celtic including the Morrígan, Hindu, and others)
can follow; see `content/language-packs/case-studies/pantheons/README.md`.
This case study is the historical dossier on **how gods were reclassified
as demons over time**, not a catalogue of ritual practice. Related:
[`../goetia/`](../goetia/) and [`../satan-lucifer/`](../satan-lucifer/)
(two distinct figures — adversary lineage and morning-star lineage —
not interchangeable names; conflation is documented there separately).

## Scope

Entry point: the *Grand Grimoire* / *Le Dragon Rouge* (the “Red Dragon”)
and the spirit hierarchy it presents. **Dating is uncertain and must stay
flagged:** the work is sometimes claimed to date from 1521 (or 1522); it
is **probably an 18th- or 19th-century** French grimoire. Do not treat the
claimed 1521 date as established.

From that late hierarchy, trace **attested** earlier sources, and keep
three layers apart:

1. **Attested philology and primary religious texts** (Hebrew Bible, LXX,
   New Testament, Ugaritic and Phoenician names, Mesopotamian spirits).
2. **Later grimoire claims** (Weyer, the *Grand Grimoire*, the Goetia,
   de Plancy, and similar). These are reception, not evidence for what
   2 Kings or an Ugaritic tablet says.
3. **Modern devotional views**, including a “dark divine” reading of
   infernal figures. That view is logged as a **modern perspective, not
   as evidence**. The center is non-biased and does not teach spell-work.

Attested traces in scope:

- Baal Zebub of Ekron (2 Kings 1) and New Testament Beelzebul (Mark 3:22,
  Matthew 12:24; cf. related synoptic wording). Include the **zebub /
  zebul debate** (Hebrew זְבוּב “fly” vs. a possible *zebul* “exalted
  dwelling / prince,” and whether *zebub* is pejorative).
- Astarte / Ashtart / Ashtoreth and the later grimoire name Astaroth.
- Molech / Moloch.
- Dagon.
- Deuteronomy 32:17 and Psalm 106:37, Hebrew *shedim* (שֵׁדִים), often
  connected with Akkadian *šēdu* — originally a protective spirit. Treat
  the etymology as a scholarly hypothesis, not as a simple identity.
- LXX Psalm 95(96):5, “the gods of the nations are *daimonia*.”
- 1 Corinthians 10:20 (sacrifices to *daimonia*).
- Greek *daimōn* (often morally neutral in classical use) and the later
  Christian sense “demon.”
- Lilith and Mesopotamian *lilitu* / *lilû* (keep Isaiah 34:14, later
  Jewish tradition, and modern Lilith readings in separate notes).
  Fuller work now lives in [`../lilith/`](../lilith/) and the draft
  pack `sumerian_lilith`.
- Pazuzu and Lamashtu as Mesopotamian **context**, not as automatic
  biblical identifications.
- Goetia and *Lesser Key of Solomon* names where a deity-name origin is
  actually attested (for example Bael ~ Baal, Astaroth ~ Astarte). Names
  without a solid basis (for example Paimon) are flagged ⚠️ UNCERTAIN.
  Fuller Goetia work lives in `../goetia/`; this case study only traces
  demotion links.
- Johann Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* (1577, PD).
- Collin de Plancy, *Dictionnaire Infernal* (PD editions).

Out of scope: evocation methods, “how to work with” a spirit, and any
other spell-work instruction.

## Method

- Date every witness and do not collapse a Bronze Age name, a biblical
  polemic, a patristic gloss, and an 18th-century grimoire into one being.
- Academic disagreement (zebub/zebul, *shedim* ~ *šēdu*, Goetia etymologies)
  is flagged, not resolved by preference.
- Every pack names its edition, licence, and the date of the witness.
- Copyrighted modern editions are REFERENCE-ONLY and will not appear in
  PDFs.
